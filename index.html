Anatomy of the house burger

Built layer by layer

Brioche crown, buttered
Shredded iceberg
Heirloom tomato
Raw-milk cheddar
Double smash patty
Toasted heel + ember sauce

Every burger starts as a loose-packed ball of chuck and brisket, ground in-house before sunrise. It hits a 700-degree plancha over live-oak embers, gets smashed once — never twice — and comes off with a lace-edged crust you can hear.

The bun is buttered and kissed on the same fire. The cheddar melts on the flat-top, not under a lamp. Ember sauce — smoked chili, charred onion, a little molasses — goes on the heel, where it belongs.

Our story

One grill.
One obsession.

Embersmith started as a Friday-night backyard ritual: a secondhand grill, a bag of live-oak charcoal, and an argument about the perfect smash.

Seven years, one food truck, and roughly forty thousand patties later, the argument is settled — and the answer is on the menu. We still cook over real embers because gas doesn't argue back. We still grind every morning because fresh beef sears differently. And we still butter both sides of the bun, because we're not monsters.

"If you can't hear the sear, it isn't done right."

— Sam Aalto, pitmaster & founder
